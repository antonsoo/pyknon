# Pyknon design context

Audience and task supplied by the owner: fluent English speakers curious about ancient musical traditions, especially Greek music, who want something substantive and surprising. The owner asks for distinctive open-source showcases rather than generic galleries.

The chosen lesson is narrow and audible: in the tetrachords attributed to Archytas by Ptolemy, changing one pitch changes the genus; rounding the enharmonic notes to a modern twelve-step keyboard merges two distinct pitches. Learners should hear the difference before confronting the ratios, then follow those ratios back to a scanned primary source.

Direction: a clear listening desk, with violet ink, warm orange playback markers, a light background and a large horizontal pitch ruler. Colour identifies the three genera; position communicates pitch. Put listening and stopping within immediate reach on phones. No autoplay, decorative waveform, simulated ancient instrument timbre, generic statistic cards or invented historical performances. Use readable labels and reversible comparisons. Greek source text gets a proper Greek-capable serif, while controls and measured values use a self-hosted sans serif.
