# Three middles in one interval

The owner's design context is in `.impeccable.md`: fluent English speakers,

This new room links musical ratio to the meaning of “number” in a Greek proof.

## Learner action

Start with 16, 17, 18. Seventeen is an arithmetic middle, but the successive
ratios 17:16 and 18:17 differ. Compare equal increments in frequency, equal
increments in ideal string length, and equal frequency ratios. Their three
middle notes differ. The exact equal split is a geometric mean.

The source is Sectio canonis III and XVI in Menge's 1916 Greek/Latin edition,
with XII–XIII for the musical ratios. Its “no middle number” claim concerns
proportional means in an argument about whole-number ratios. It does not remove
real-valued geometric means or prohibit a modern oscillator from approximating
one. The integer candidate and the continuous model remain separate.

Scale both endpoints and the candidate together. The ratio does not change;
more integers can appear between the endpoints without supplying the needed
proportional mean. Try both integer neighbours of the geometric mean, then the
4,6,9 control, whose 9:4 ratio is outside the superparticular premise.

## Exact account and sound

Integers A < B are numerical weights, realized as modern frequencies relative
to A. Their values do not claim measured ancient hertz or string lengths.
For candidate M, compare M/A with B/M through M² versus AB. Arithmetic and
harmonic candidates are reduced rational numbers. The geometric candidate is
sqrt(AB), with exact symbolic equality and a disclosed floating-point audio
realization. No rounded frequency decides the equality sign.

An ideal string comparison uses f proportional to 1/L with speed held fixed.
Equal length increments therefore give the harmonic mean of the endpoint
frequencies, rather than their arithmetic mean. Three listening modes compare
the halves from a common starting pitch, the complete low/middle/high path,
and the two ending tones together. A same-tone control holds oscillator count
and gain constant. Reuse the finite Player/WAV graph, no instrument timbre,
autoplay, hearing score or recovered performance.
