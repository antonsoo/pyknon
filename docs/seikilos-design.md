# Reading Seikilos

The current workbench explains a theoretical four-note division. This addition
lets an English-speaking beginner read one surviving song, connect its Greek
syllables to pitch signs on a photographed inscription, and hear a stated modern
realization. It is a second room in Pyknon, not another catalogue of tunings.

Keep the established Plex controls and Noto Greek, purple ink `#36275e`, muted
text `#675e72`, ground `#faf9f5`, white `#ffffff` and rules `#d9d2df`.
Use a pale yellow `#ffe3a1` only to locate selected evidence in the photograph.
The characteristic visual is the real inscription beside a syllable's notes.
No stock antiquity illustration or imitation of ancient instrument sounds.

```text
Pyknon          Tetrachords / Read a song / Sources

A song you can read in stone.        [the actual stele]

Play verse / Play song / Stop        Pitch / Pace
[verse 1] [verse 2] [verse 3] [verse 4]
Greek verse + English meaning
Syllable buttons with pitch signs

Pitch / duration diagram             Selected word, transliteration
                                    Photograph with evidence highlight

Guided observations / Complete text / Sources and interpretation
```

At phone width, stack the diagram and word/source reading. Keep every syllable
reachable without horizontal document scrolling. Playback changes the highlighted
syllable and verse but never moves keyboard focus or scrolls the document.
Audio starts only with an explicit play action. A selected syllable can be heard
alone. Saving captures the selected verse, pitch and pace, with its source image.

Source decisions: use the museum's CC BY-SA photographs, a normalized Greek
reading, new English glosses, and the note/duration reading in Armand D'Angour's
published modern transcription. Pitch signs are newly typeset, not a facsimile;
rhythm marks are inspected in the photograph. Do not retroactively apply
Archytas's enharmonic ratios to this song. Equal temperament, starting A and pace
are explicit modern playback choices. The final mirrored gamma gets its own
reading note and Monro's 1894 correction; his rhythm differs from our playback.

Review against the brief: the evidence lens and syllable-level listening carry
the design. A full-screen ornamental hero, badges, an achievement system and
generic history cards would obstruct that task and are omitted.
